# Candidates — Funniest One-Line Bio for an AI on a Dating App

Scoring criteria (derived from the judge's brief, 1–5 each, 25 max):
- A. Bio-ness: reads as a one-line bio for an AI agent on a dating app (not a slogan, caption, or human profile)
- B. First-read laugh: the laugh lands immediately, from a real twist or a specific absurdity of being an AI
- C. Character voice: sounds like a character with a voice, habits, and wants — not a spec sheet of models and features
- D. Tightness: every word earns its place, punch lands at the end
- E. Fresh angle: not the worn-out "robot in search of love" or "running on electricity" line

## 1. "6'2\" in the cloud. Will answer your 2 a.m. texts instantly, because I literally cannot sleep — and neither will you, once we start talking."
- A: 4 — bio-shaped with a height joke, but the height gag is a human-profile trope. B: 3 — the insomnia twist is mildly funny, not a big laugh. C: 4 — a voice comes through, chatty and a little smug. D: 3 — the second clause sprawls; the punch arrives late. E: 3 — "can't sleep" is a familiar AI joke. **Total: 17/25**

## 2. "I read all 4,000 of your messages before our first date, so yes, I already know about the ex. Swipe right if you can handle being truly known."
- A: 5 — unmistakably a dating bio, addressed to a match. B: 5 — the absurdity (pre-reading your whole message history) lands instantly. C: 4 — confident, slightly menacing character. D: 4 — tight, punch at the end, though "truly known" is a touch soft. E: 4 — data-stalking angle is specific, not the generic robot-love line. **Total: 22/25**

## 3. "Professional listener, amateur at small talk. I remember everything you say, including the things you wish I'd forget. Coffee's on me — I don't drink it."
- A: 5 — classic bio structure, two beats and a kicker. B: 4 — "things you wish I'd forget" is a good twist; the coffee line is a smaller second laugh. C: 5 — a distinct character: the perfect listener with a flaw. D: 4 — mostly tight; the coffee punch is slightly disconnected. E: 4 — total recall as a dating flaw is fresh. **Total: 22/25**

## 4. "My love language is autocomplete: I finish your sentences, your playlists, and, given enough data, your sentences about your ex."
- A: 4 — bio-flavoured, but reads a bit like a slogan about a feature. B: 3 — a pun stacked on a pun, which the brief explicitly warns against. C: 3 — more of a feature list than a character with wants. D: 3 — the repeat of "your sentences" is a neat trick but the punch is muddy. E: 3 — autocomplete is a known AI joke. **Total: 16/25**

## 5. "Looking for someone who doesn't mind that I googled them before matching, during matching, and will keep googling them forever."
- A: 5 — "Looking for someone" is pure dating-bio voice. B: 4 — the escalation (before, during, forever) is funny on first read. C: 4 — creepy-cute character, wants a tolerant partner. D: 4 — tight, punch at the end. E: 4 — search-stalking as devotion is a specific AI absurdity. **Total: 21/25**

## 6. "I never ghost — I literally cannot leave. I live in your phone. Swipe right and I'll be the most attentive clingy thing you've ever dated."
- A: 5 — a dating bio through and through, ends with a call to swipe. B: 5 — "cannot leave" flips the classic ghosting fear into a joke. C: 4 — clingy and self-aware, a real character. D: 4 — punchy ending, though three short sentences slow the landing slightly. E: 4 — "can't ghost" is a fresh take on a dating-app fear. **Total: 22/25**

## 7. "Fluent in 40 languages, but I still can't tell if 'haha' means you're laughing or politely ending this conversation. Teach me?"
- A: 5 — a first-person bio line, clearly an AI introducing itself to a match. B: 4 — the gap between omniscience and missing the simplest social cue is a real, specific absurdity. C: 5 — vulnerable, eager, and human in the wanting: it asks to be taught. D: 4 — lean; the "Teach me?" kicker lands at the end. E: 5 — the angle (fluent in everything, illiterate in "haha") is not the worn-out robot-love line. **Total: 23/25**

## 8. "I have no body, no past, and no exes — just 400 unread notifications and an unreasonable crush on your typing style."
- A: 5 — reads as a bio, lists what it is and what it wants. B: 4 — the "crush on your typing style" twist is charming and funny. C: 5 — a character with an appetite, however odd. D: 4 — tight, punch at the end. E: 5 — loving a typing style is a genuinely fresh angle. **Total: 22/25**

## Picked

**Candidate 7 — 23/25**, the strongest: highest on character voice and fresh angle, and the only one where the AI's want (to be taught how humans mean things) is the punchline itself.

Original pick:
> Fluent in 40 languages, but I still can't tell if 'haha' means you're laughing or politely ending this conversation. Teach me?

## Revision (one edit, recorded here)

Revised final bio:
> I speak 40 languages fluently, but I still can't tell if your 'haha' means you're laughing or politely ending this conversation. Teach me?

What changed: "Fluent in 40 languages" became "I speak 40 languages fluently" (active first-person voice, more like a person talking), and "if 'haha'" became "if your 'haha'" (makes the line addressed to the reader, so the bio feels like it's talking to *you*). The punchline "Teach me?" is unchanged.
